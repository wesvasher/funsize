## The short answer

Leave SwiftCut's volume slider at **0 dB**, the level the ear button sets. Set how loud that is in the room **once**, with your Mac's own volume, and leave it there whenever you edit.

## Why 0 dB

The volume slider above the video changes only what you hear. It never changes your exports or the meters. At 0 dB, you hear exactly what will be in the exported file.

If you turn the slider down, a clip that's too quiet can still sound fine. Your ears judge loudness against what they're used to, so if the listening level moves from day to day, you can't compare clips by ear.

Use the slider when you need to, for example when someone is talking to you. Then click the ear button to go back to 0 dB.

> The ear button turns blue when you're at the monitoring level.

## Set your Mac's volume once

1. In SwiftCut, click the ear button so the slider is at 0 dB.
2. Play a clip that's already at your loudness target. Use one that reads close to the target in the Selected Clip meters with Loudness on in Timeline Settings, or any well-made podcast or online video.
3. Turn your Mac's volume until speech sounds like a normal conversation with someone about a meter (3 feet) away. That's roughly 70 to 75 dB of sound where you sit.
4. Note that volume step and use it whenever you edit.

A free sound meter app on your phone, or the Noise app on Apple Watch, can check the level where you sit.

## The levels studios use

Studios set their speakers so a reference sound measures a fixed level where the editor sits:

| Kind of work | Typical loudness target | Listening level |
|---|---|---|
| Cinema (large room) | — | 85 dB SPL |
| TV and broadcast | −23 or −24 LUFS | about 79 dB SPL |
| Small room, speakers close by | — | 73 to 79 dB SPL |
| Online video and podcasts | −14 or −16 LUFS | about 70 to 75 dB SPL |

Online video is mixed louder, so it sounds bigger at the same speaker volume. That's why its listening level is a little lower.

## Headphones

The same idea applies. Pick a comfortable level once, with the slider at 0 dB, and keep it. Listening loud for long sessions tires your ears and makes everything sound a little duller, so keep it moderate.
